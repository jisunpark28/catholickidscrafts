## Overview

**Duration:** 35–45 minutes  
**Ages:** Grade 2–4 (adapt for family catechesis)  
**Topic:** The Holy Trinity — **three Divine Persons, one God**

Saint Patrick is said to have used a **three-leaf clover** (shamrock) to teach people in Ireland about the Trinity. The clover does not *explain everything* about God—no picture can—but it helps children **begin** to wonder at a great truth of our faith.

![Diagram: three leaves on one stem labeled Father, Son, Holy Spirit, and One God on the stem](/resources/holy-trinity/trinity-clover-hero.svg)

## Learning goals

Children will be able to:

- Name the **three Divine Persons**: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit  
- Say that Christians believe in **one God**, not three gods  
- Use the clover as a **reminder**: many parts, **one** living plant—like three Persons, **one** God

## The clover analogy (say it simply)

Hold up a real clover, a paper shamrock, or point to the picture.

> The clover has **three leaves**, but we do not call it “three clovers.”  
> We call the whole plant **one shamrock**—**one** clover.

> In the same way, God is **Father**, **Son**, and **Holy Spirit**.  
> We do **not** worship three gods. We worship **one God**—three Persons, perfectly united in love.

![We say one shamrock, not three separate leaves](/resources/holy-trinity/clover-analogy.svg)

### Important note for catechists

Tell children clearly:

- The clover is a **helpful symbol**, not a perfect picture of God.  
- The Trinity is a **mystery** we believe because Jesus revealed it and the Church teaches it.  
- We **adore** one God in three Persons.

## Materials

- Printed hero poster or coloring page (links below)  
- Green construction paper, scissors, glue (optional craft)  
- Crayons or markers  
- Optional: fresh clover from the yard

## Activity 1 — Color and label (15 minutes)

1. Give each child the **coloring page**.  
2. Color the three leaves. Label **Father**, **Son**, **Holy Spirit**.  
3. Color the stem and write **ONE GOD** in bold.  
4. Practice saying together: *“Three Persons, one God.”*

[Download coloring page](/resources/holy-trinity/trinity-coloring.svg)

## Activity 2 — Paper shamrock craft (15 minutes)

1. Cut three heart-shaped “leaves” from green paper.  
2. Glue them at one point to form a shamrock.  
3. Glue a strip of paper as the **stem**.  
4. On the back, write the Sign of the Cross slowly:  
   *In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.*

## Sign of the Cross connection

Every time we pray the Sign of the Cross, we name the **three Persons** and bless ourselves in the name of the **one God**. Repeat it three times slowly so children feel the rhythm of the words.

## Discussion questions

1. How many **leaves** does the clover have? How many **clovers** is the plant?  
2. How many **Persons** are there in God? How many **Gods** do we believe in?  
3. Who are the three Persons? (Father, Son, Holy Spirit)

## Closing prayer

*God our Father, Jesus our Lord, Holy Spirit our helper—  
we believe you are three Persons and one God.  
Teach us to love you with all our heart. Amen.*

## For parents & teachers

- Pair with the **Apostles’ Creed** line: “I believe in one God…”  
- Avoid comparing the Persons to “modes” or “roles” only—the Church teaches each Person is fully God.  
- For older students, add that the Son became man in Jesus Christ; the Spirit is given to the Church at Pentecost.
